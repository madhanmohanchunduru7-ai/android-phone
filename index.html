About the Project

Objective: to understand how different sections of society use Android phones — which apps they rely on, how many hours they spend, and what problems they face — and to spread awareness about healthy, safe and productive usage.

Outcome: a survey-based report, awareness sessions for the community, and practical recommendations on screen time, digital safety and useful apps.

Project Details

Advantages, disadvantages and health issues of using Android mobile phones.

✅ Advantages

  • Instant communication through calls, WhatsApp and video calls.
  • Easy digital payments (UPI) and banking.
  • Online learning, e-books and study material anywhere.
  • Maps, weather, news and government services at hand.
  • Helps farmers and shopkeepers with market prices and customers.
  • Entertainment, camera and affordable price range of Android phones.

⚠️ Disadvantages

  • Addiction to social media, reels and games.
  • Loss of study and work time (distraction).
  • Online frauds, fake links and OTP scams.
  • Privacy risk from unnecessary app permissions.
  • Less face-to-face interaction with family and friends.
  • Cyber-bullying and exposure to unsuitable content.

🩺 Health Issues Caused by Mobile Use

Eye strain

Dry, tired eyes and blurred vision (digital eye strain).

Neck & back pain

“Text neck” from looking down for long hours.

Sleep problems

Late-night use and blue light reduce sleep quality.

Headache & stress

Too much screen time, notifications and anxiety.

Thumb & wrist pain

Repetitive typing and scrolling strain.

Less physical activity

Sitting for long hours may cause weight gain.

Tips: follow the 20-20-20 rule (every 20 minutes look 20 feet away for 20 seconds), keep phone away at bedtime, use Digital Wellbeing limits and take regular breaks.

Our Conclusion

Our CSP study shows that the Android phone has become a part of everyone’s daily life, but each group uses it differently. Students spend the most time, mainly on videos, games and social media. Working adults use it for work, payments and communication. Homemakers use it for family calls and videos. Senior citizens use it mostly for calls and video calls, and shopkeepers and farmers rely on it for UPI payments and market information.

The phone is a powerful tool for learning, business, banking and staying connected. However, too much use causes eye strain, neck pain, poor sleep, stress and exposure to online fraud. The problem is not the phone itself but the way and the time for which it is used.

Recommendation: use the phone with balance — set daily screen-time limits, take breaks, avoid it before sleep, protect personal information and OTPs, and teach elders and children safe digital habits. Used wisely, the Android phone can improve life instead of harming it.
